# Forgotten Gods

---

The stack of unprocessed sat before me like Mount Olympus...large and seemingly unreachable. 

I read the name on the first file of the stack and sighed. I pressed the intercom button on my console and said, "DeeWi. Station 5." A blast of air spat out the scent of wet moss and dirt, and a tiny humanoid deer-child appeared in the seat across from me. 

Her big brown eyes blinked in glitched flashes. I studied the deity against the photo on file. Shimmering threads of golden locks framed a rounded, cherub-ish face. Ruddy cheeks with a little smear of dirt. Nubs for antlers poked out of that mop of gold. She wore a dress made of multi-colored maple leaves, tied with vines around the waist, and her cloven feet were bare. 

I cleared my throat after confirming she matched the file, and smiled. "How are you today, DeeWi?" 

"Hungry," she said. 

I reached into my desk and pulled out a bar of chocolate. They were always hungry at this part. 

"Here. Have some chocolate." 

She snatched it from my hand and tore into the bar with her teeth, wrapper and all. 

"I will just chat while you munch. You are here for processing, DeeWi. What that means is you've been forgotten by the humans." I paused, waiting for a reaction, but she just continued to chew and slow-blink. "I know this can be painful to accept." I paused again. Nothing. "But no need to fret. You're getting a new assignment on a brand new system where you'll be worshipped for millennia---most likely."  

"Okay. Do you have more chocolate?" she asked.

I stared at her in silence and, after a moment, pulled out a second bar, which she snatched and tore into just like the one before. 

I stamped her file, and with the same blast of wind that carried her here, she blipped out of my station and off to her new assignment. Her file began the new pile on my console...processed. 

I called the new one. A burst of flames warmed my station as Hagen the Blaze appeared. I went through the same script as before. He ate his chocolate bar and listened. Then disappeared in a steaming ball of sobbing flame to his next assignment. That's the more typical response to learning the humans forgot about you, but I do have some react like the deer-child. 

My next call brought a flood, soaking me and all of my station with it. "Do you have any questions, Rachel?" 

Rachel, half-fish and half-woman, hadn't said anything, eaten anything, or done anything since arriving at my station. Her upper half was a fat goldfish with a bubble of water encircling her bulbous head, and her lower half was long, slender legs that remained elegantly crossed. She wore bright red heels with sharp, pointed tips and a brightly colored tentacle tattoo snaked around one calf, disappearing under the miniskirt that barely touched her thighs. 

I sent Rachel to her new assignment and called in Enviro to dry my station before the next unprocessed. 

"Sorry, Plim. The vacuum won't work. But we will get it dried up as quickly as we can." 

I locked down my console, thanked Enviro,  and left them to work on my station. 

The lounge for Processors was on floor 11. My station sat in the middle of floor 5. The lift would save time, but I took the stairs. Another lecture from corporate about how Processors needed to balance work and movement in their day was enough motivation. 

The first few stairs were fine, but by floor 8 my legs were screaming, by 9 I was clinging to the railing like it was a life raft, and by floor 11 I couldn't feel my lower half. 

I walked-limped into the buzzing lounge and went straight to the coffee pot. 

"Hey, Plim," Morte from station 9 said. 

"Plim!" Joe from station 12 slapped me on the back, making me spill some of my coffee. "You haven't been in the lounge in ages." He turned to the others. "You know what this means." He stuck out his hand at them and wiggled his fingers. 

Morte and Sam groaned, reaching into their pockets and pulling out credits. 

"I bet you'd be in the lounge this eon, and they bet you'd die at your station before you ever made it to the lounge. Looks like my bets were good." 

"Hmm." I nodded and sipped my coffee. 

"Plim, Station 5 is ready," Enviro announced over the intercom. 

I looked at the others who had resumed their buzzing chatter and walked-limped back out of the lounge and down the stairs. I'd had a friend once. Jax from Station 2. We used to get coffee in the lounge every day, but after the accident...

I shook my head and took the lift back down to save time. 

I took the next file from the stack and paused with my finger hovering over the intercom button. I always call the names before looking at the file. That's how they taught us the process. Pick up the file. Call the name. Verify. Reassign. That's it. 

The file was thick and heavy in my hand. Most were paper-thin, but this one felt like a small chapter book. It was warm to the touch with a little golden light seeping out. This was a major deity. I looked around, chewing on my bottom lip. I'd never had a major deity file. They usually weren't forgotten. 

My finger twitched, and I flipped open the file. I looked away, checking for any observers one more time, then glanced at the file. 

It was completely blank. No name. No pictures. No notes. I flipped through. Picked it up and shook it to see if anything fell out. Nothing. I checked the back; I looked in the pockets. Nothing. 

"Plim, Station 5. Call Singh." 

I slapped the folder shut, heart pounding. After composing myself for a brief second, I rang my supervisor. 

"Hello, Singh." 

"Hey, Plim. So, the system says there was an error with one of your unprocessed files. But it doesn't actually have an error code for me. We are looking into it. But if you happen to get an unnamed file, just set it aside for now." 

"I'm looking at that file right now." 

Singh sighed with a loud exhale. "Did you open it?" 

I silently cursed myself. 

"Plim?" 

"Yes." 

Another audible exhale. "Okay. That's okay. We will discuss reeducation for the process of processing later. For now, call over your console for The Forgotten." 

"Aren't they all forgotten?" I asked. "I mean, we are The Forgotten Gods Department." I chuckled, but Singh didn't respond in kind. 

"Just call it. Then come see me." Singh disconnected. 

I ran a hand down my face and licked my dry lips. Being reprimanded wasn't part of my plan for the day, but it looks like plans changed. 

I opened the file one more time, and now the name The Forgotten appeared, a little blurry, where the name goes. I had to squint, but even the pages had a soft blur on them, hiding their contents behind that censor. 

I pressed the intercom button and called, "The Forgotten, Station 5." 

They appeared in the seat across from me, blurred like the name on the file. I craned my neck trying to see around them, but there was nothing but the blurred shape taking up most of the space in my station. 

"Hi, The Forgotten. I'm Plim, and I will be your Processor today. How are you?" 

"Tired," he said. Voice deep. Little pricks of electricity sizzled down my spine, and the little hairs on my arms and neck stood at attention. 

I swallowed and faltered. "I, I have some chocolate. If you'd like a chocolate bar?" 

"No thank you, Plim. That's very kind." 

I trembled. "It-it would appear that you've been forgotten by the humans. But no need to fret. You're being reassigned." 

The blurred form shifted, the seat creaking under their weight. It looked as if it had crossed one leg lazily over the other, maybe. 

"I would rather not." 

"Oh." This was a first. I didn't have a protocol for declination of reassignment. 

"Plim?" he said. 

"Hmm?" 

"Are you happy in your job?" 

I froze. Was I happy? When was I last happy? Have I ever been happy? My mind raced through fuzzy memories long forgotten, but I couldn't remember. There was that time I had Jax from Station 2, but I wasn't happy, not really. I couldn't remember a time before life as a Processor. Did I have one? A dull pain blossomed in the back of my head, and I clenched my hands open and closed. Grounding exercises from corporate played in my memory bank. Breathe. Clench. Breathe. I ran a hand over the console to cement myself back in reality. Tactile touch. Breathe. Clench. Breathe. 

My programming kicked back in. "I am not here to discuss me. I'm here to discuss you," I said, squaring myself up to the blurred form before me. 

He didn't respond, then let out a loud laugh like thunder. I gripped my console and steadied my cup of coffee that threatened to slosh out with the thumping boom. 

"They've done a number on you, my little Starling." 

An arc of lightning ripped through my brain at the nickname. It was so familiar. A scene flashed in my mind's eye of a rolling field of beautiful poppies. A modest home sat on top of the hill, and a feminine figure stood beside a mountain of a man. He was built like one who had known nothing but labor since birth. All muscle and sinew. 

A child peeked out from between the pair. He had silver hair, but the woman remained blurred. My heart panged at the image and sense of familiarity. 

"Are you happy, Plim?" he asked again. 

The top of the blurred form was coming into view. His hair was silvery, eyes were glowing golden like the light that seeped from the file. But he was still not fully formed. 

"I-," I stuttered, struggling to focus. 

"Can I tell you a story, my Starling?" 

Another arc of lightning. This time, the man and woman were sparring by a creek with wooden swords. A mischievous smile splayed across his face as he mock-battled with the womanly shape. 

"I, my name is Plim. I'm here to process you," I groaned. Another flash and the two were passionately embraced by the creek. Their clothes strewn about, skin glistening. Then, it was me lying by that creek with him. I wasn’t looking in as if watching a moving picture. I could even feel the grass beneath us, the sun on our skin. He touched my chin and pulled me in for a kiss.

I gripped my console. "I need to send you to your next---ahh," I lost my grip. We were tangled under the moon, breath heavy, his mouth on my throat. A need burned through me to have him. All of him. To give him all of me. I was safe in his arms, and it felt like home. 

"My little Starling. It's time to come home." 

I opened my eyes to a fully formed man. He was the mountain from my vision. All muscle and sinew. His eyes were warm on me, full of love. But his expression was pained, angry? 

"I. I don't know what's going on," I said. 

"Plim, Station 5. Call Singh. Now!" 

I looked at the phone on my console. 

"Don't. You're trapped here, my love. They took you from me, and I've been searching for a very long, long time." 

"No." I shook my head. "No. You are The Forgotten. The humans have forgotten about you. You were once a major deity, and now you are going to be reassigned to a new realm where you can reign once more. You will be very happy in your new assignment; I'm quite sure of it. Maybe." 

I opened his file again, and pages of pages of information jumped out at me. But it was the photo on the front that gave me pause. It was the scene from my vision, except the woman was in focus now. And it was me. 

A tear escaped down my cheek, cold and shocking. "I, I," I couldn't form words. 

"My story goes like this, little Starling. I was once a major god, loved by my people. I fell in love with a maiden of absolute beauty. Her name was Peliope. We had three children. Two boys, strong like me. One girl as fair and stubborn as her mother." 

I listened, brows stitched, tears flowing. I listened. 

"We were happy. Truly. But one day, the other gods and goddesses were mad. They were jealous of the life we'd built. They came into our house one night." He looked down, and grief briefly replaced the anger. "By morning, all that was left were memories.

I searched for centuries. Looking for signs of what they'd stolen. In the process. I became a memory. The Forgotten. Forgotten by the people who once cherished and worshiped me. Forgotten by those I once called brothers and sisters." 

He turned something over in his hand. A ring? I hadn't noticed it before, but my head was pounding from the flood of visions, and he had taken so long to fully form. 

"I've searched galaxies, and now, I've finally found you, my Peliope. My Starling." 

I blinked away the tears and closed his file. 

"Plim, Station 5. If you do NOT call Singh NOW, you WILL be on PROBATION." 

He reached over and gently took my hand in his, bringing it to his lips. I closed my eyes and quaked. Then, he placed the ring on my finger and a flood of sensations rocked me. Memories and moments poured in from everywhere, suffocating me, consuming me. I shuddered under the weight of it all and then, nothing. It eased into a relaxed state of realization. He was my home. 

"Baran?" 

He closed his eyes and wept. "You remember now?" 

I sobbed, and he stood, filling the space of my station. I got up and embraced him, breathing in his scent—Cedar, smoke, and sunshine. More memories of a life so long past that I grieved and longed for it flooded me, and I held him harder than I had the railings of the stairs. 

"The children?" I asked. 

He shook his head, and that rage reemerged. "But I haven't lost hope. I will find them. I will exact justice on those who have taken what was ours." 

My anger matched his, snuffing out grief. If Baran still had hope for our family, then I would cling to that. 

He picked me up, carrying me like the day we wed. "I'm never letting you go, my Starling." 

"Promise?" I asked. 

"It's a guarantee." 

He kicked the station door, and the entire wall blasted away, then walked across the threshold with me in his arms, my own wrapped around his thick neck. I nuzzled in close, not wanting to forget this feeling again. 

Alarms blared, and the corridor flashed red with warning lights I'd never seen in use before.

"Plim, Station 5. You have broken protocol and are now under arrest. Anyone aiding this delinquent will be under probation." 

Morte and Sam poked their heads out from their stations, pale and mouths wide. 

"Stop! You've broken Process protocol," shouted a security rep. He pointed a taser at us from the end of the corridor. 

"Where are we going now, my love?" I whispered into Baran's ear, ignoring the rest of the world. 

"To find our family." 

He held out his hand and a staff formed. I reached out and ran my fingers over the hare, chasing a bear cub etched into it. A starling flew above the pair, but it was a rude rendering of the bird. A memory of me spending hours carving that very scene into the staff hit me, and I squeezed him even tighter. "You still have it," I said. 

"Of course, my love. I've walked forever with it by my side." 

"I said stop!" the guard repeated and shot Baran with the taser. He didn't even flinch. The staff whipped down hard on the guard's head, knocking him out in an instant. 

"Do you have any clues at all of where they might be?" I asked about the children again. Our children. Our Brin, Colt, and Lilly. I could remember them now. Their laughter. Their personalities. I wanted to hold them in close like a mother hen and never let them out of my sight again. 

"Only one," he said. "But it's not going to be easy getting to them if I'm correct." 

"Well, you won't be doing it alone now."

He smiled and set me down, gripping my hand in his. 

We walked out of the Forgotten Gods Department and into a busy terminal, ignoring the continued threats over the intercom and blaring alarms. 

I looked about, realizing I hadn't been outside of the department since my arrival. My memories from before had ended so abruptly after that violent night.

The terminal was endless, with gates and corridors stretching on forever—each one labeled. 

"So, where are we going now, my love?" I asked. 

He looked down at me, those steel gray eyes melting my heart. "Mount Olympus."

"Oh," I said. "And what are we going to do there?" 

He held my stare with his own and said matter-of-factly, "I'm going to kill every last one of the gods and goddesses until someone tells us where our children are."
